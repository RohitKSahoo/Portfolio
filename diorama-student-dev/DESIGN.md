DESIGN.md --- Student Developer Bedroom Diorama
1. Design Intent
Create a stylized 3D isometric cutaway diorama of a student developer's
personal bedroom and workstation. The room should feel lived-in,
creatively messy, warm, and technically expressive---a compact space
where studying, software development, gaming, and everyday life overlap.

The visual direction follows a handcrafted miniature-room aesthetic: -
Warm timber construction and furniture. - Muted teal walls with a
turquoise environment backdrop. - Rich amber practical lighting
contrasted with cool monitor light. - Dense but intentional prop
placement. - Rounded, slightly exaggerated low-poly forms with polished
materials and soft shadows.

The room should read clearly as a miniature physical set, not a flat
illustration or a realistic architectural render.
2. Overall Composition
Diorama format
- One square wooden platform forms the room base.
- Two perpendicular walls meet at the rear corner, creating an
  open-front, open-top cutaway.
- Expose the entire interior from a fixed elevated isometric camera.
- Use a thick, visible timber frame along the top edges of both walls.
- Keep the front and one side open so the desk, chair, bed, shelving,
  and floor clutter remain visible.
- Add a subtle bevel to the platform edges and visible layered wood
  grain.
Camera
- Orthographic isometric camera.
- Elevated three-quarter view, looking down into the room.
- Rear corner sits near the upper-centre of the composition.
- Show the full platform with a small amount of breathing room around
  it.
- Avoid perspective distortion and avoid cropping any major furniture.
- Use a clean turquoise studio background and a soft contact shadow
  beneath the diorama.
3. Room Architecture
Walls and structure
- Two interior walls in muted desaturated teal / blue-green.
- Exposed dark honey-brown timber beams along the top edges and rear
  corner.
- Timber posts and braces can be visible where they support the
  structure.
- Add subtle wall panel seams and restrained surface variation.
- Keep the wall height sufficient to frame the workstation without
  hiding the monitor setup.
Floor and platform
- Warm walnut / oak plank flooring.
- Individual floorboards with slight colour and grain variation.
- Raised wooden platform with a thick layered edge.
- Small scuffs and darker seams to reinforce the miniature handcrafted
  look.
- A textured rug anchors the chair and workstation area.
Window
- Place a wide rectangular window on the left wall.
- Use a dark timber frame and horizontal wooden venetian blinds.
- Warm daylight enters through the slats, creating distinct diagonal
  light bands across the desk and floor.
- Keep the window bright but not blown out.
4. Primary Workstation
The workstation is the visual focal point and should occupy the
back-left / central region of the room.
Desk
- Large L-shaped wooden desk fitted into the rear corner.
- Thick timber desktop with visible grain, slightly rounded corners,
  and sturdy legs.
- One main surface for the monitors and keyboard, with a secondary
  return surface for notebooks and accessories.
- Open leg space must remain visible so the chair reads clearly.
- Cable routing and a few hanging wires should be visible beneath the
  desk.
Triple-monitor setup
- Three monitors arranged in a shallow arc.
- Centre monitor faces the camera most directly; left and right
  monitors angle inward.
- Thin dark bezels, compact stands, and subtle screen reflections.
- Each screen displays a distinct developer workflow:
  - Left: code editor with syntax-highlighted source files.
  - Centre: terminal, IDE, or active code workspace.
  - Right: browser preview, logs, or debugging output.
- Use small, legible-looking code lines and UI panels as visual
  texture; do not rely on readable text at miniature scale.
- Cool blue, cyan, violet, and green screen accents should contrast
  with the warm room lighting.
Input devices and desk accessories
- Mechanical keyboard with individual keycaps and restrained RGB
  illumination.
- Mouse and large dark desk mat.
- Two compact desktop speakers, one on either side of the monitor
  arrangement.
- Headphones hanging from a wall hook or monitor-side stand.
- Ceramic mug, water bottle, phone, USB drives, and small charging
  accessories.
- Open notebook with handwritten-looking marks and a pen.
- Loose paper sheets and sticky notes, arranged messily but kept clear
  of the keyboard and screens.
Developer details
- Add a small rubber-duck debugger near the monitor stand.
- Include a few small circuit boards, adapters, cables, and a USB hub.
- A compact desk lamp with an adjustable arm provides a warm pool of
  task light.
- Show a few cables crossing or dropping behind the desk, but avoid
  turning them into visual noise.
5. PC Tower and Hardware
- Place a desktop PC tower beneath the right side of the desk.
- Use a dark charcoal case with a transparent side panel.
- Show two or three illuminated cooling fans through the panel.
- Add restrained RGB rings in violet, cyan, and blue.
- Include a subtle internal GPU / motherboard silhouette.
- Keep the tower visible from the camera angle; do not bury it behind
  the chair or desk legs.
- Optional: add a small power strip and a few neatly tangled cables
  near the rear desk leg.
6. Seating and Floor Zone
Ergonomic chair
- One prominent ergonomic swivel chair in front of the desk.
- Dark grey frame, mesh or fabric back, padded seat, armrests, central
  column, and five caster wheels.
- Use a muted accent colour on the seat or lumbar support, such as
  dusty coral or desaturated blue.
- Angle the chair slightly toward the centre monitor.
- Keep its silhouette readable and do not let the chair block the PC
  tower.
Rug
- Medium-sized rectangular rug under the chair and desk zone.
- Dark charcoal / muted slate base with a subtle geometric or woven
  pattern.
- Slightly softened edges and visible pile texture.
Floor clutter
The floor should feel naturally messy, not randomly filled: - Backpack
leaning near the desk or bookshelf. - A small stack of textbooks and
notebooks. - One or two loose sheets of paper. - Headphone or charging
cable near the chair. - Pair of sneakers near the front edge. - A small
laundry basket with clothes peeking out. - A game controller and a
couple of game cases near the rug. - One takeaway cup or snack wrapper
as a minor lived-in detail.

Keep a clear visual path through the room; clutter must not obscure the
main furniture.
7. Bed and Rest Area
- Place a single bed along the right wall.
- Use a simple wooden bed frame with a visible headboard and legs.
- White or warm cream pillow and bedsheet, with a dark navy /
  slate-blue blanket casually folded and partly rumpled.
- Include a small bedside table with a warm glowing lamp, a book
  stack, and a small potted plant.
- Add storage boxes or drawers beneath the bed if visible from the
  camera.
- The bed should feel used and comfortable rather than perfectly
  staged.
Cat
- A small orange-and-white tabby cat is curled up asleep on the bed.
- Rounded stylized body, tucked paws, curved tail, and closed eyes.
- The cat is an ambient storytelling detail, not a focal character.
- Give it a soft contact shadow on the blanket.
8. Storage, Shelving, and Wall Decor
Left bookshelf
- Tall wooden shelving unit against the left wall, near the window.
- Mix upright books, horizontally stacked books, small storage boxes,
  and a few collectibles.
- Add trailing green plants on the upper shelf and smaller pots at
  lower levels.
- Include a skateboard or longboard leaning beside the shelf if space
  allows.
- Avoid perfectly aligned books; vary their heights and orientations.
Right-side shelf
- Compact wooden wall shelf near the bed.
- A few books, headphones, small gadgets, and a plant.
- Keep the arrangement asymmetrical and casually maintained.
Pinboard
- Large corkboard or warm cork-coloured panel above the desk on the
  right wall.
- Pin up architecture sketches, flow diagrams, handwritten notes,
  small printed references, and coloured sticky notes.
- Use a mixture of paper sizes and slightly rotated placements.
- The board should imply an active student project workspace without
  requiring readable text.
Posters and personal touches
- One or two framed art prints above or beside the workstation.
- Use restrained retro-futurist, space, game-art, or abstract
  graphics.
- Add a few small collectibles or desk toys.
- Avoid excessive branding or prominent real-world logos.
9. Materials and Colour Palette
Primary palette
  Element                      Colour direction
  Walls                        Muted teal / blue-green
Timber frame and furniture   Warm walnut, honey oak, dark brown
Floor                        Medium warm oak
Desk accessories             Charcoal, graphite, muted neutral tones
Chair accent                 Dusty coral or desaturated blue
Bedding                      Cream, white, navy, slate blue
Plants                       Natural varied greens
Background                   Turquoise / teal gradient
Material treatment
- Timber: semi-matte, visible grain, gently bevelled edges.
- Walls: matte painted finish with subtle tonal variation.
- Metal: dark satin finish on chair hardware and monitor stands.
- Plastic: soft satin finish on electronics.
- Screens: emissive surfaces with controlled bloom.
- Fabric: visibly soft, slightly textured rug and bedding.
- Ceramic: gentle highlights on mugs and pots.
- Foliage: layered leaves with varied green tones.
10. Lighting and Rendering
Lighting setup
- Warm key light from the window, filtered through the blinds.
- Warm desk-lamp and bedside-lamp practical lights.
- Cool, low-intensity monitor glow on nearby objects.
- Soft ambient fill to preserve detail in shadowed corners.
- Gentle rim light to separate the room silhouette from the
  background.
Render style
- High-quality stylized 3D miniature / low-poly diorama.
- Soft global illumination and ambient occlusion.
- Soft, directional shadows with visible contact beneath objects.
- Slightly exaggerated bevels and rounded edges.
- Detailed materials without photorealistic noise.
- Warm-cool lighting contrast, controlled highlights, and no harsh
  clipping.
- Clean studio presentation with no text labels, UI overlays, borders,
  or watermark.
11. Composition and Visual Priority
Maintain this hierarchy: 1. Primary focal point: Triple-monitor
developer workstation. 2. Secondary focal point: Ergonomic chair and
illuminated PC tower. 3. Tertiary focal point: Bed with sleeping
cat. 4. Supporting details: Window, shelving, pinboard, plants,
books, and floor clutter.

The room should look busy when inspected closely but remain instantly
readable at thumbnail size. Preserve negative space around the chair and
desk so the workstation does not become visually congested.

13. Scope and Animation Readiness
This specification defines the static hero diorama shown in the
reference image. It does not require a human character, courier,
delivery sequence, AI orbs, or a build-success animation.

If animation is added later, preserve the composition and use small,
local movements: - Monitor content scrolls or changes subtly. - PC fans
rotate and RGB lighting pulses slowly. - Desk and bedside lamps maintain
a gentle warm glow. - Cat breathes subtly and flicks its tail
occasionally. - Window light and blind shadows remain stable to avoid
distracting motion.

The static scene must remain visually complete and appealing even when
every animated element is idle.
